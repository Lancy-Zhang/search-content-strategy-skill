# Plan Keywords & Pages
## Input
Use brand, market, keyword/query list and sources. Add existing URLs, GSC data and dated keyword-tool estimates when available. If no list is supplied, generate candidate terms from verified brand context and label them as candidates.
## Process
1. Confirm core products, audience, use cases and business goal.
2. Preserve original terms and sources. Create a cleaned field for case/whitespace and remove exact duplicates. Flag irrelevant terms with reasons. Preserve meaningful modifiers such as location, model and size.
3. Group terms by user need. Similar expressions may share one group; different needs may require different pages even when their broad intent is the same.
4. Review representative search results for the specified market/language. Record date, URLs, predominant page formats, primary/secondary intent and confidence. Without results, give provisional intent only.
5. Map groups to existing pages and assess whether content and format meet the need. Multiple keywords can map to one page. Multiple pages on a topic do not automatically prove keyword cannibalisation; check actual overlap and performance.
6. Recommend create, update, candidate merge, or defer. For a merge candidate, identify the retained URL, content migration and redirect/internal-link checks; do not delete pages automatically.
7. Pass actions to prioritisation with measurement suggestions.
## Deliver
Original/cleaned term; source/market; group and user need; primary/secondary intent; search-result evidence; optional volume/difficulty with tool/date; existing URL; target page/action; rationale; measurement.
## Checks
Distinguish Google Ads targeting keywords from actual search terms/queries. SEO candidate terms are not proven demand. Unknown volume is unknown, not zero. Do not compare estimates from different markets/tools as equivalent. Avoid one article per keyword.
